# Accessible Film, TV and Video Content Production Management: human-managed content creation and production activities and prompts

**Prepared:** 8 November 2026 · **Source revision:** `9f9a157270f9087c59b2ab17b54fc166a76d9d7e`  
**Source:** [Double-FW/Accessible-Film-TV-and-Video-Content-Production-Management](https://github.com/Double-FW/Accessible-Film-TV-and-Video-Content-Production-Management/tree/9f9a157270f9087c59b2ab17b54fc166a76d9d7e)  
**Audience:** authors, editors, designers, publishers, communication teams, producers, commissioners, access-service practitioners and technical/quality partners.

## Where the repository is useful

Use this repository to commission and coordinate accessible audiovisual productions across creative development, filming, post-production, access services, technical delivery, audience review and archive. The main production guide and two cross-repository maps clarify how producers work with caption and audio-description specialists rather than treating accessibility as a late file order.

The main guide is practitioner guidance with editorial diagnostics, historical claims and incomplete jurisdiction-specific applicability. The AGENTS production workflow is a draft for local adoption, not an operating production agent or automatic gate system. Source requirements and example mappings must be tailored to exact cut, language, destination, territory and audience. Keep receipt, validation, human acceptance and published playback verification separate.

The activities below are an editorial application of the repository, not a claim that it already contains a complete organisational process. AI can organise material, draft options and scripts, identify inconsistencies and prepare specifications or evidence summaries. Authors and editors preserve factual meaning and editorial intent. Designers and technical practitioners implement and verify delivered formats. Qualified access-service specialists review their work, and affected audiences inform the experience. Accountable publishers/producers retain creative, acceptance, rights and release decisions. Assign these responsibilities to actual people locally; the examples do not establish a new team or approval body.

## How to use the prompts

1. Attach [RAG_Reference.md](RAG_Reference.md). For selective retrieval, use the source routes below and [Source_Coverage.md](Source_Coverage.md).
2. Replace bracketed inputs with the actual project brief, artefacts and evidence. Supply the target task, audience, platform/setting, constraints and intended decision. Treat missing information as unknown.
3. Use either example prompt for the activity. Both refer to this folder’s reference. Supply only appropriate project information; use anonymised research findings where possible.
4. Require source ID, filename and original section citations; distinguish source guidance, local proposal, observed result and uncertainty. Ask for evidence requests where an artefact cannot be inspected.
5. Review the result with the named human roles and record decisions. A prompt output is a draft editorial/production aid, not implementation or evidence that a test ran. Do not defer disabled people’s involvement until final testing.

**Reusable response contract:** “Use the attached RAG_Reference.md as the knowledge source. Cite source IDs, files and sections. Distinguish source statements, project evidence, recommendations and unknowns. Preserve applicable source identifiers. Do not invent research, capability, approvals or test results. Give a concrete content/production artefact, trade-offs, evidence requests and decisions for the human team.”

## 1. Commission a versioned accessible production

**Production stage:** Commissioning and production planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** Scope and limits; section 7; AGENTS: Scope and commissioning.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Define production identity, audience, creative intent and release variants.
- Identify services and specifications needed for each destination.
- Budget, schedule and assign access-production responsibilities early.

**Expected output:** A commissioning brief and release-variant/access-service matrix.

**Human review:** The accountable producer confirms scope, resources and actual requirements with specialists and distributors.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the attached RAG to commission [production and intended releases]. Define audience, creative purpose, cut/version, languages, territories, aspect ratios, live/prerecorded status and destinations. Plan required access routes, specialist input, schedule, resource questions and acceptance evidence. Leave current platform or regulatory requirements unverified until supplied or checked.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [production brief] using the RAG. Find access services added after edit lock, every service assumed necessary for every version and destinations with no confirmed specification. Return a revised planning matrix and producer decisions.

## 2. Plan sound-led and supplementary access routes

**Production stage:** Treatment development and editorial planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Audio Description](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 2; Production/AD mapping: commission and assess.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Audit what visual meaning the soundtrack already provides.
- Consider integrated narration/commentary before separate AD.
- Record service choices and unresolved timing or language conflicts.

**Expected output:** A proposed sound-access treatment and audit/decision plan.

**Human review:** The director, editor and access specialists agree treatment; a route decision is verified against the finished cut.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG and its AD mapping, plan sound access for [format, script and treatment]. Distinguish main-audio explanation, separate AD, extended/described versions and text alternatives. Identify visual information, emotion and on-screen text that need an equivalent and plan final-cut verification; do not decide no AD from genre alone.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [access-route proposal] with the RAG. Identify a podcast-style treatment assumed to cover all visual meaning, missing silent reactions or no room for essential description. Propose production changes and specialist questions without inventing a completed audit.

## 3. Design graphics, framing and sign-language access together

**Production stage:** Pre-production and visual design

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 3–4, 6 and 10.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Plan readable essential text, charts and language access.
- Coordinate caption regions, signer framing and graphics.
- Assess cognitive clarity and motion without flattening creative intent.

**Expected output:** A graphics/framing access plan and production annotations.

**Human review:** Graphics, camera, director, signing and caption specialists verify the actual treatment with relevant audiences.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to review [storyboard, graphics and framing plan]. Coordinate essential on-screen text, caption space, signing visibility, speaker identification, cognitive clarity and motion. Specify equivalent audio/text routes and unresolved language choices. Distinguish sign language from captions and avoid assuming one signed language serves all audiences.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [creative access layout] against the RAG. Find captions covering a signer, graphics too brief to use or essential information present in only one channel. Propose coordinated alternatives and specialist review evidence.

## 4. Prepare capture, sound and live access operations

**Production stage:** Production and live planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S005 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Closed Caption Subtitles](RAG_Reference.md#s005); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 1, 5–7; caption mapping: Capture and preparation.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Capture intelligible speech and useful source material.
- Provide scripts, names, terminology and changes to access teams.
- Plan competent live monitoring, delay and fallback where relevant.

**Expected output:** A capture/sound handoff and live-access rehearsal brief.

**Human review:** Production, sound and access-service leads confirm actual operating arrangements before live use.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, plan [shoot or live event] for intelligible dialogue and reliable access-service capture. Identify microphones/mixes, scripts, speaker names, specialist terms, changes, access practitioners, rehearsal, monitoring and fallback. Do not treat captions as a fix for an unnecessarily unintelligible main mix.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [production/live access plan] using the RAG. Identify missing source materials, no update route for script changes and live fallback that leaves viewers uninformed. Return handoffs and practical rehearsal questions, not invented operational approvals.

## 5. Coordinate post-production access assets

**Production stage:** Editing and access-service management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Audio Description](RAG_Reference.md#s004); [S005 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Closed Caption Subtitles](RAG_Reference.md#s005).

**Relevant source sections:** Sections 1–3 and 7; both cross-repository mappings.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Connect exact picture/sound versions to captions, AD and transcripts.
- Resolve collisions, translation, timing and intelligibility.
- Reassess affected access work after creative or technical changes.

**Expected output:** An access-asset dependency schedule and post-production handoff.

**Human review:** The editor and access practitioners agree matching versions and editorial decisions; specialist work is not replaced by a generated draft.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG’s production/AD and production/caption mappings to coordinate [post-production package]. Define source cut, audio, language, cue/timecode basis, access deliverables, text/transcript relationships and review owners. Identify collisions and version changes that invalidate earlier work. Keep detailed specialist questions routed to the relevant source.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [post-production access handoff] against the RAG. Find captions tied to the wrong audio, an AD stem mistaken for a complete mix and a picture change with no affected-asset review. Produce corrected dependencies and decisions.

## 6. Commission specialist QA and audience evaluation

**Production stage:** Production review and acceptance planning

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003); [S004 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Audio Description](RAG_Reference.md#s004); [S005 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Closed Caption Subtitles](RAG_Reference.md#s005).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 8; mapping gate evidence; AGENTS: Review and gates.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Separate editorial/technical QA from audience outcome evaluation.
- Tie findings and recommendations to actual variants and contexts.
- Preserve failed, unresolved and inapplicable states with evidence.

**Expected output:** A scoped QA/evaluation brief and human gate recommendation.

**Human review:** Qualified practitioners assess access assets; the accountable producer decides any production transition within real authority.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, plan QA and audience evaluation for [production variants]. Separate specialist access-service review, technical checks and disabled audience experience; define evidence, scope and unresolved issues. Use the production source’s verified/failed/unresolved/not applicable meanings carefully, with recorded rationale. Leave unperformed checks unresolved rather than passed.

### Example prompt 2

> Audit [production gate pack] against the RAG. Identify internal checks called audience validation, unknown applicability treated as a pass and approval asserted without delegation. Return a version-specific recommendation and required human decisions.

## 7. Verify delivery, discovery and published playback

**Production stage:** Distribution and publication management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 9; Minimum pre-delivery checklist; AGENTS: release.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Confirm actual destination formats, metadata and controls.
- Test selected services and version relationships through publication.
- Keep delivery receipt separate from successful audience access.

**Expected output:** A delivery/published-playback test plan and evidence record.

**Human review:** Distributor/platform and production owners verify authorised playback paths; outstanding publication checks stay visible.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to prepare delivery and publication checks for [destinations and asset versions]. Record current specification inputs, formats, languages, labels, metadata and player controls. Plan actual discovery, selection, seeking/resume and relevant device checks after publishing. Distinguish receipt, validation, acceptance and observed published access.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [delivery acceptance record] using the RAG. Find a local master treated as evidence of a published transcode, missing AD/caption labels or untested selection persistence. Produce a scoped verification plan and explicit outstanding items.

## 8. Control changes, decisions and archival relationships

**Production stage:** Production maintenance and redelivery

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessible_TV_Content_Guidance_for_Production_Teams.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Audio Description](RAG_Reference.md#s004); [S005 — Cross-Repository Accessibility Mapping: Production Management and Closed Caption Subtitles](RAG_Reference.md#s005); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** Section 7; cross-repository archive/redelivery stages; AGENTS: archive.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Retain masters, access assets, specifications, rights and review history.
- Assess redelivery and revision impacts across linked assets.
- Record decisions without claiming unconfigured enforcement or retention rules.

**Expected output:** An archive/version manifest proposal and redelivery impact brief.

**Human review:** Production and rights/records owners determine authorised retention and reuse; changes need appropriate re-review.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, plan archive and redelivery for [production package]. Link masters, access assets, scripts, languages, destination specifications, rights references and review evidence by exact version. Identify records/access arrangements to confirm locally and revision triggers that require new editorial, technical or audience checks.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [proposed re-edit or redelivery] against the RAG. Identify stale captions/AD, missing rights context, orphaned evidence and earlier acceptance carried into a changed platform. Produce an affected-asset/retest list and human decisions.

## Editorial and production decision record

For each adopted result, record the project/task, source revision and sections used, observed editorial/production issue, barrier or opportunity, chosen option and rejected alternatives, expected outcome, evidence available, assumptions, unresolved constraints, accountable person, next check and review trigger. Use actual names and dates when agreed; leave them unassigned when unknown. Record acceptance and residual risk only when the accountable person has made the decision.

Revisit the reference when repository sources change, a new platform or context is introduced, implementation changes invalidate assumptions, or user feedback exposes a barrier. Compare changed sources rather than silently replacing the basis of existing decisions.
